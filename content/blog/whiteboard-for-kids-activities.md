Children love a whiteboard. There is something about a big empty surface and bright markers that makes them want to draw, write and explain. An online whiteboard on a tablet or computer keeps that feeling, with a few extras: there is no ink on the sofa, every drawing can be saved, and the board never fills up.

Here are twelve activities that work well for kids aged roughly 3 to 10. They need no preparation beyond opening a board. On SiteWhiteboard there is no account and nothing to install, which matters when children use the device.

## Before you start

- **A tablet works best.** Kids draw naturally with a finger. A stylus is a nice bonus for older children.
- **Pick a thick marker.** Thin lines are frustrating for small hands. Choose the thickest size.
- **Show them undo.** Knowing that a mistake can disappear with one tap makes children braver.
- **Sit with them for the first few minutes.** After that, most kids are happy to explore alone.

## Drawing games

### 1. Finish the picture

Draw half of something: a circle with two ears, a car with no wheels, a house with no door. Ask your child to finish it. Then swap roles. This builds imagination and gets reluctant drawers started.

### 2. Draw what I say

Give simple instructions one at a time: "Draw a big circle. Put two small circles inside. Add a smile. Now give it three legs." The result is usually funny, and children practise listening carefully.

### 3. Colour hunt

Ask your child to draw five things that are red, then five things that are green. Younger kids learn colour names, and older kids can add the name of each object with the text tool.

### 4. Shape monsters

Use the shape tool to drop a few rectangles, circles and triangles on the board. The challenge: turn them into a monster by adding eyes, arms, teeth and hair with the pen. Every monster is different.

## Letters and numbers

### 5. Trace the letters

Write a large letter in light grey and let your child trace it in a bright colour. Do their name one letter at a time. Because the board is endless, you can practise a whole alphabet without running out of room.

### 6. Counting stickers

Write a number on a sticky note, then ask your child to draw that many objects next to it: three apples, seven stars, ten balls. Check together by counting out loud.

### 7. Simple sums with pictures

Draw two groups of objects, such as three cats and two cats, with a plus sign between them. Your child counts them all and writes the answer. Later, swap the pictures for numbers. Seeing the objects first makes addition concrete.

### 8. Missing number

Write a number line from 1 to 10 with a few numbers missing. Kids fill in the gaps. For older children, count in twos, fives or tens.

## Stories and imagination

### 9. Story in four boxes

Draw four boxes in a row. Your child draws a story: beginning, problem, solution, ending. Then they tell the story out loud while pointing at each box. This is a gentle way to practise sequencing, which helps with reading and writing.

### 10. Map of my room

Ask your child to draw a map of their bedroom from above. Where is the bed? The door? The window? Then hide a toy and draw an X on the map for them to find. Maps help develop spatial thinking, and kids love treasure hunts.

### 11. Draw a feeling

Ask "what does happy look like?" or "what colour is angry?" There is no wrong answer. This activity gives younger children a way to talk about feelings without needing the words.

### 12. Picture and caption

Paste a photo onto the board, such as a family photo, a pet or a picture from a trip. Your child draws on top of it, adds speech bubbles and writes a caption. It turns a photo into a story they made.

## For teachers and parents together

Many of these activities work in a classroom too. Put the board on the projector and let children come up one at a time to add to a shared drawing. Activities 2, 7 and 9 work especially well with a group.

A whiteboard is one of the simplest creative tools a child can use. Start with "finish the picture" and see where their imagination goes.
