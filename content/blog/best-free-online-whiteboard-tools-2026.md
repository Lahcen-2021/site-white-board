There are more free online whiteboards than ever, and they are not all trying to do the same thing. Some are built for teams working together in real time, some for designers, some for classrooms and some for one person who wants to sketch without signing up.

Google Jamboard used to be a common answer to "which free whiteboard should I use?" Google shut it down at the end of 2024, which sent many teachers and teams looking for a replacement. This list covers the main free options in 2026, what each is good at and where each falls short.

We make SiteWhiteboard, which is on the list. We have tried to describe every tool, including our own, honestly.

## How we compared them

We looked at what matters most when choosing a free whiteboard:

- **Setup:** do you need an account or an install?
- **Collaboration:** can several people draw on the same board at once?
- **Drawing tools:** pen, shapes, sticky notes, text, images
- **Devices:** does it work on a phone and tablet with touch and pen?
- **Free plan limits:** what you lose if you do not pay

Free plans change often. Check each tool's own website for the current limits before you commit.

## Excalidraw

Excalidraw is a free, open source whiteboard with a distinctive hand-drawn look. It is very popular with developers for architecture diagrams and quick sketches. You can start without an account, and a share link lets others join a live session.

**Good for:** technical diagrams, quick sketches, people who like the hand-drawn style.
**Less good for:** handwriting-heavy work such as maths, and classroom use with young children.

## tldraw

tldraw is another open source whiteboard with a clean interface and smooth drawing. The tldraw.com site lets you draw and share boards for free. It handles pen input well and feels fast.

**Good for:** freehand sketching, quick collaborative boards, people who like a minimal interface.
**Less good for:** people who need templates or structured workshop tools.

## Microsoft Whiteboard

Microsoft Whiteboard is free with a Microsoft account and is built into Microsoft Teams meetings. For schools and companies that already use Microsoft 365, it is the natural choice because nothing else needs to be set up.

**Good for:** Teams meetings, Microsoft 365 schools and companies, pen input on Windows devices.
**Less good for:** anyone without a Microsoft account, or who wants to draw without signing in.

## FigJam

FigJam is the whiteboard from the makers of Figma. It is fun to use, with stickers, stamps and widgets, and it fits naturally into a design team's workflow. Figma offers a free starter plan and free education plans for verified students and teachers.

**Good for:** design teams, product workshops, people already using Figma.
**Less good for:** quick anonymous use, since you need an account.

## Canva Whiteboards

Canva includes whiteboards inside its design platform. If you already use Canva for slides and posters, you can brainstorm on a board and turn it into a design in the same place.

**Good for:** Canva users, visual brainstorming that ends in a designed document.
**Less good for:** a quick board without an account, and heavy handwriting.

## Miro (free plan)

Miro is one of the biggest whiteboard platforms. Its free plan gives you real-time collaboration and access to a large template library, with a limit on how many boards you can edit. We compare it in detail in [Online whiteboard vs Miro vs Mural](/blog/sitewhiteboard-vs-miro-vs-mural/).

**Good for:** teams that collaborate on boards every week and want templates and integrations.
**Less good for:** quick one-off use, and people who cannot or do not want to create an account.

## SiteWhiteboard

SiteWhiteboard is a free online whiteboard that opens in one click. There is no account and nothing to install. You get markers in several colours and sizes, an eraser, sticky notes, shapes, arrows that stay attached to shapes, text, images and PNG export. Boards are saved in your browser while you work and are never uploaded to our servers. It works with a mouse, a finger or a pen on phones, tablets and computers.

There is no live collaboration. A board lives on one device, and you share it by exporting a picture or by showing your screen. The free plan includes up to three boards, and a paid Pro plan removes ads and the board limit.

**Good for:** teaching on a projector, explaining on a shared screen, students, kids, quick sketches without signing up.
**Less good for:** several people editing the same board at the same time.

## Which one should you pick?

There is no single best tool, only the best tool for the job in front of you.

- **For real-time teamwork**, pick Miro, FigJam or Microsoft Whiteboard, depending on what software your team already uses.
- **For technical diagrams**, Excalidraw is hard to beat.
- **For teaching, tutoring and explaining on a shared screen**, a fast board with no sign-up, such as SiteWhiteboard or tldraw, keeps the focus on the lesson.
- **For students and kids**, avoid anything that needs an account. SiteWhiteboard and Excalidraw both work without one.

Most people end up using two tools: a collaborative platform for team projects and a fast, simple board for everything else. Try a couple of the options above for a real task, not a demo, and keep the one that gets out of your way.
