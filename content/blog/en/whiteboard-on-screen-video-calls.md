Some ideas are hard to explain with words alone. A process with five steps, the layout of a page, the structure of a database, a maths problem. In a room you would stand up and draw it. In a video call you can do the same thing by opening an online whiteboard and sharing your screen.

This guide shows how to set it up in Zoom, Google Meet and Microsoft Teams, and how to make your drawings easy to follow for everyone watching.

## Why share a whiteboard instead of using the built-in one?

Zoom, Teams and other meeting apps have their own whiteboard features. They are worth knowing about, especially when you want everyone to draw together. But a separate browser whiteboard has some advantages:

- it looks and works the same in every meeting app, so you learn one tool
- you can prepare the board before the call, with images and notes already in place
- the board is saved in your browser after the call, and you can export it as an image
- the other people in the call do not need any account or extra software

The idea is simple: you draw, everyone else watches your shared screen.

## Before the call

1. Open a board in a separate browser window, not a tab in the same window as other work.
2. Make the window a comfortable size. If you have two monitors, put the board on one and the meeting on the other.
3. If you already know what you will draw, set up the starting point: a title, a few sticky notes, a diagram or a screenshot pasted with Ctrl+V.
4. Close notifications and other windows you would not want others to see.

## Sharing in Zoom

1. Click **Share Screen** in the meeting controls.
2. Choose the browser window with the whiteboard, not the whole desktop. This keeps the rest of your screen private.
3. Click **Share**.
4. Draw. Everyone sees your strokes as you make them.

If people are watching on phones, zoom in on the part of the board you are working on so the lines are big enough to read.

## Sharing in Google Meet

1. Click **Present now** (the screen icon at the bottom).
2. Choose **A window** or **A tab**. Sharing just the tab with the board is the cleanest option.
3. Select the whiteboard and click **Share**.

Google Meet compresses video more aggressively on slow connections. Thicker lines and larger text stay readable even when the picture quality drops.

## Sharing in Microsoft Teams

1. Click **Share** in the meeting toolbar.
2. Under **Window**, choose the browser window with the board.
3. Draw while you talk.

Teams also has Microsoft Whiteboard built in, which lets every participant draw. If your colleagues need to edit the board too, that built-in option may suit the session better. For one person explaining, sharing a separate board is often quicker.

## Drawing tips for video calls

**Use a pen or touch screen if you have one.** A tablet with a stylus makes handwriting much clearer. You can keep the meeting on your laptop and join the same call from the tablet as a second device, then share the tablet's screen while you draw on it.

**Go thick and bright.** Video compression blurs thin, light lines. Pick the middle or thick marker size and strong colours like blue, red and black.

**Talk while you draw.** Say what you are drawing as you draw it: "This box is the website, and this arrow goes to the database." Silence while you draw feels long to people watching.

**Zoom to the action.** A board full of small details is hard to read on a laptop screen, and harder on a phone. Zoom in on the part you are explaining and zoom out only to show the overview. The fit button frames the whole board in one click.

**Use shapes and arrows for diagrams.** Hand-drawn boxes are fine, but the shape tool (S) gives clean rectangles and diamonds, and arrows stay attached when you move a box. You can rearrange a diagram live without redrawing it.

**Use sticky notes for decisions.** When the group agrees on something, put it on a sticky note in a corner of the board. At the end you have a list of decisions everyone saw being written.

## After the call

Export the board as a PNG and drop it into the meeting notes, the team chat or a follow-up email. A picture of the diagram everyone saw is often clearer than a written summary. The board also stays in your browser, so you can continue it in the next meeting on the same device.

## Common problems

- **Others see a black screen.** On macOS, the meeting app needs screen recording permission in System Settings under Privacy and Security. Grant it and restart the app.
- **The drawing lags.** Share a single window rather than the whole screen, and close heavy tabs.
- **People cannot read your text.** Zoom in on the board, use bigger text and thicker markers.

A shared whiteboard turns a call from people talking past each other into people looking at the same picture. Try it at your next meeting the moment someone says "it's hard to explain."
