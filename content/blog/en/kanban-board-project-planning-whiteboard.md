Project management software can be overwhelming. Fields, statuses, labels, sprints, permissions. For many projects, especially personal ones or small teams, you need much less. A whiteboard with three columns and some sticky notes is enough to see what needs doing, what is in progress and what is done.

That setup is called a kanban board. This guide shows how to make one on an online whiteboard and how to use it so it actually keeps a project moving.

## What is a kanban board?

Kanban comes from Japanese and roughly means "signboard" or "visual card." The method was developed in manufacturing at Toyota and later adopted by software teams. The idea is simple: every piece of work is a card, and the cards move across columns as the work progresses.

The basic board has three columns:

- **To do**: work that is planned but not started
- **Doing**: work in progress right now
- **Done**: finished work

That is it. Everything else is optional.

## Setting up the board

Open a new board and draw three tall rectangles side by side with the shape tool (S). Label them "To do," "Doing" and "Done" with the text tool (T), or put the labels on large sticky notes at the top.

Leave the "To do" column wider than the others, because it usually holds the most cards.

If you want, add a title above the board with the project name and the deadline. A clear goal at the top helps everyone decide which cards matter.

## Writing good cards

Each card is a sticky note with one piece of work. On SiteWhiteboard, double-tap an empty spot or press N to create a note, then type.

A good card:

- **starts with a verb:** "Write the intro," "Call the printer," "Fix login bug"
- **is small:** something that can be finished in a day or two
- **is clear to someone else:** avoid private shorthand

If a card feels too big, split it. "Build the website" is a project, not a card. "Design the home page" and "Write the About text" are cards.

## Using colour

Colour turns a simple board into a more useful one. Pick a meaning and stick to it:

- one colour per person, so you see who is doing what
- one colour per type of work, such as design, writing and admin
- red notes for urgent or blocked work

Do not use too many colours. Three or four are plenty.

## Moving the cards

The rules are simple:

1. When you start a task, drag its card from "To do" to "Doing."
2. When you finish, drag it to "Done."
3. Pick the next card from the top of "To do."

Keep the "To do" column sorted by priority, most important at the top. Then "what should I do next?" never needs a meeting. It is the top card.

## Limit work in progress

This is the rule that makes kanban powerful, and the one most people skip. Set a maximum number of cards allowed in "Doing" at once. For one person, two or three is a good limit. For a small team, try one or two per person.

Write the limit at the top of the column, like "Doing (max 3)." When the column is full, you cannot start anything new until you finish something.

Why does this help? Because starting work feels productive, but finishing it is what delivers results. A board with twelve cards in "Doing" and nothing in "Done" is a team that is busy but not moving.

## Add a "Blocked" area

Sometimes a card cannot move because you are waiting for someone or something. Do not leave it in "Doing," where it hides the problem. Add a small "Blocked" area under the "Doing" column and move the card there, with a note saying what it is waiting for.

Look at the blocked cards first in every review. Unblocking them is often the most valuable thing you can do.

## The weekly review

Once a week, spend fifteen minutes with the board:

1. Celebrate the "Done" column, then clear it. Export the board first if you want a record of completed work.
2. Check "Doing." Is anything stuck? Should it be split or blocked?
3. Re-sort "To do" by priority. Remove cards that no longer matter.
4. Add new cards for the coming week.

On SiteWhiteboard the board is saved in your browser, so it is waiting for you each time you open it on the same device. Exporting a PNG at each weekly review gives you a simple history of the project.

## Kanban for different projects

**A school project:** columns for research, writing and checking, with cards for each section of the report.

**A personal goal:** a kanban for moving house, planning a wedding or learning a language. It turns a big stressful goal into small visible steps.

**A small team:** share your screen in the weekly meeting and move the cards together. Each person says what they finished, what they are doing and what is blocking them.

**Content planning:** columns for ideas, drafting, editing and published. This is how many bloggers plan articles.

## When to move to dedicated software

A whiteboard kanban is perfect for personal projects and small teams that meet regularly. If your team needs everyone updating cards at the same time from different places, with due dates, attachments and notifications, a dedicated tool such as Trello, Jira or a collaborative whiteboard will serve you better.

But many projects never reach that point. Three columns, a handful of sticky notes and the discipline to finish before starting will take most projects a long way.
