Most brainstorming sessions follow the same script. Someone writes the question at the top of the board, people call out ideas, and after ten minutes the same three voices have filled the space. The board itself is not the problem. The method is.

Here are ten ways to use a whiteboard that get more ideas out of a group, or out of your own head when you are working alone. Each one works on a wall whiteboard, and each one is easier on an online whiteboard where space is unlimited and notes can be moved around.

## 1. The classic mind map

Write the topic in a circle in the middle. Draw a branch for every main theme, then smaller branches for ideas inside each theme. Mind maps work because they show connections, not just a list.

On a digital board, use a different marker colour for each main branch. When a branch grows too big, drag the view over and keep going. There is no edge to hit.

## 2. Sticky note silent storm

Give everyone five minutes to write ideas on sticky notes, one idea per note, without talking. Only then put them all on the board.

Writing in silence stops the loudest person from setting the direction. It also produces far more ideas, because nobody is waiting for their turn. On SiteWhiteboard, double-tap an empty spot to create a note and type straight into it.

## 3. Affinity grouping

After a silent storm you have a pile of notes. Now drag similar ideas next to each other and name each group with a short label. Patterns appear that nobody would have said out loud: three people wrote different versions of the same idea, or a whole cluster of notes is about a problem nobody planned to discuss.

This step is where an online board saves the most time. Moving fifty paper notes on a wall is slow. Dragging them on a screen is not.

## 4. Crazy eights

Draw a grid of eight boxes. Set a timer for eight minutes and sketch one idea per box, one minute each. The time pressure stops people from polishing their first idea and forces them to keep going.

Crazy eights comes from design sprints and works best for visual problems: a page layout, a logo direction, the shape of a product. The sketches can be ugly. That is the point.

## 5. Reverse brainstorming

Instead of asking "how do we solve this?", ask "how could we make this worse?" How could we make sure no customer ever finishes the sign-up form? How could we make this lesson as boring as possible?

People find bad ideas easy and fun to suggest. Once the board is full, go through each one and flip it. "Ask for twenty fields of personal details" becomes "ask only for an email address."

## 6. Starbursting

Draw a six-pointed star. Write the idea in the middle and put one question word on each point: who, what, when, where, why and how. Then fill each point with questions, not answers.

Who will use this? What does it replace? When would someone need it? Starbursting is useful when an idea already exists and you want to find the holes in it before you spend money or time.

## 7. Round robin sketching

Each person starts a sketch or a sentence on their own part of the board. After two minutes, everyone moves one spot to the right and builds on what the previous person started. Repeat until the ideas come back to their owners.

In a remote meeting where only one person controls the board, run it as round robin talking instead: each person adds one thing to the previous idea while the facilitator draws.

## 8. The 2x2 matrix

Draw two lines that cross to make four boxes. Label the horizontal axis "effort" (low to high) and the vertical axis "impact" (low to high). Place each idea from your session where it belongs.

The top-left box, high impact and low effort, is where you start. The bottom-right box, low impact and high effort, is where ideas go to quietly die. The matrix turns an hour of brainstorming into a short list of next steps.

## 9. Worst possible idea warm-up

Before the real session, spend three minutes collecting the worst possible ideas for the problem. Write them all on the board in a corner.

It sounds like a waste of time. It is not. Once people have said something ridiculous out loud and nobody judged them, they share their real ideas much more freely. Some of the bad ideas also turn out to contain a good one.

## 10. Picture prompts

Paste a few unrelated images onto the board: a city street, a kitchen, an airport, a toy. Ask "what could our problem learn from this?" A restaurant queue might suggest a better way to handle support tickets. A toy might suggest a simpler onboarding.

On an online whiteboard, paste images with Ctrl+V or drag them in. Write the ideas next to each picture with a different colour.

## Tips for any brainstorming session

- **Write the question, not the topic.** "How might we get more students to finish homework?" gives better ideas than "Homework."
- **Separate generating from judging.** First collect, then sort, then decide. Mixing the three kills ideas early.
- **Keep the raw board.** Export a PNG of the messy board before you clean it up. Sometimes the rejected idea becomes useful a month later.
- **End with owners.** A brainstorm with no next step is just a nice meeting. Put a name on a sticky note next to each idea you will act on.

## Brainstorming alone

All of these methods also work for one person. A mind map or a silent storm with yourself, followed by grouping, is one of the fastest ways to get unstuck on an essay, a business plan or a design problem. An online whiteboard helps here because you can leave the board, come back the next day and keep building on it. In SiteWhiteboard the board is saved in your browser while you work, so it is still there when you return on the same device.

Pick one technique you have not tried before and use it at your next meeting. Reverse brainstorming and the silent storm are the two that change a session the most for the least effort.
