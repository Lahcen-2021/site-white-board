Search for "online whiteboard" and the same two names come up again and again: Miro and Mural. Both are large collaboration platforms used by product teams, consultants and agencies. They are also more than many people need when all they want is to sketch an idea or explain something on screen.

This article explains what each tool is built for and where a simple browser whiteboard such as SiteWhiteboard fits in. We make SiteWhiteboard, so we will be upfront about where the bigger tools are the better choice.

## The short answer

- **Miro** is a large visual workspace for teams. Its strengths are real-time collaboration, a huge template library and many integrations with other work software.
- **Mural** is also a team workspace, with a strong focus on running workshops: facilitation features help a host guide a group through an activity.
- **A simple online whiteboard** like SiteWhiteboard is for one person drawing quickly: no account, nothing to install, boards stored on your own device.

If several people need to edit the same board at the same time from different places, Miro or Mural is the right kind of tool. If you need to draw something now, on your own or on a shared screen, a simple whiteboard is faster.

## Miro in more detail

Miro is built around an infinite canvas that a whole team can work on at once. You see other people's cursors moving, you can comment on items and you can start from templates for retrospectives, user journey maps, roadmaps, org charts and many other formats. Integrations with tools like Jira, Slack and Google Drive make it part of a team's daily workflow.

The cost of all this is weight. You need an account, invitations have to be managed, and there is a learning curve before a new user feels at home. For a team that lives on boards every day, that cost is worth it.

## Mural in more detail

Mural also offers a shared canvas and templates, but it leans towards structured sessions. Facilitation features help a host run a workshop: guiding attention, timing activities and collecting votes. Consultants, trainers and design teams who run many sessions with groups often prefer it for this reason.

Like Miro, Mural needs accounts and is designed for organisations. It shines when someone is actively facilitating a group activity.

## Where a simple whiteboard fits

Many whiteboard moments are not team workshops at all:

- a teacher explaining a maths problem on the classroom projector
- a student making a mind map before an exam
- a developer sketching an architecture during a video call while sharing the screen
- a parent drawing with a child on a tablet
- a manager drawing a quick org chart to paste into an email

In each case one person draws and others, if any, watch. Here the account, the invitations and the template gallery get in the way. SiteWhiteboard opens straight to a blank board. You get markers, sticky notes, shapes, arrows that stay attached, text, images and PNG export. Boards are saved in your browser as you go, and nothing is uploaded to our servers.

## Privacy and data

This is a real difference that is easy to miss. In Miro and Mural, boards live in the company's cloud, which is what makes collaboration and access from any device possible. Organisations usually manage this through their admin and security settings.

In SiteWhiteboard the board never leaves your device. That is good for privacy and for anyone who cannot sign up for tools, such as many students. The trade-off is that you cannot open the same board on another device, and if you clear your browser's site data the board is gone. Export anything important as a PNG, or save the board as a project file.

## Which one should you choose?

Ask yourself three questions.

**Do several people need to edit at the same time from different places?** If yes, choose a collaborative tool like Miro or Mural.

**Will you run structured workshops with a group regularly?** If yes, look closely at Mural's facilitation features, and compare them with Miro's.

**Do you mostly draw alone, teach, or sketch while sharing your screen?** If yes, a simple online whiteboard will be quicker every time, with nothing to set up.

Plenty of people use both kinds. A team might plan its quarter in Miro, while a team member still opens a quick blank board to sketch something during a one-to-one call. The tools do not compete as much as the search results suggest.
